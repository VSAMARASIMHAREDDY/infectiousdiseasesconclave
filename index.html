Infectious Diseases and Global Health

Basic definition of infection refers to the harm caused by pathogenic microorganisms such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, parasites, or other infectious agents entering the human body. Some infections are mild, while others can be severe or life-threatening depending on the type of organism involved. From understanding the causes, symptoms, pathogen detection, clinical studies, treatment strategies, infection prevention and control, antimicrobial resistance, vaccine development, novel diagnostic technologies, and innovative therapeutic approaches, participants can discuss the latest scientific advancements at our upcoming Infectious Diseases Conference.

The global infectious diseases market continues to witness remarkable growth due to the increasing burden of emerging and re-emerging infections, rising antimicrobial resistance, expanding vaccine development, growing healthcare investments, and continuous advancements in diagnostic technologies. In 2025, the global infectious diseases therapeutics market was valued at approximately USD 172 billion and is projected to reach nearly USD 255 billion by 2030, growing at a CAGR of around 8.2% during the forecast period. Simultaneously, the infectious disease diagnostics market is valued at over USD 35 billion and is expected to grow steadily with the increasing adoption of molecular diagnostics, point-of-care testing, and AI-powered diagnostic platforms. North America continues to dominate the global market due to its robust healthcare infrastructure and strong research investments, while the Asia-Pacific region is anticipated to witness the fastest growth owing to increasing healthcare expenditure, improved disease surveillance, and expanding access to advanced diagnostic and treatment technologies.

Viral Infections

Viral infections are caused by viruses that invade the body's cells and can range from mild, self-limiting illnesses to severe and life-threatening diseases. While many common viral infections resolve within 7–10 days with adequate rest, hydration, and supportive care, chronic viral diseases such as HIV, Hepatitis B and C, and emerging viral infections require long-term antiviral therapy and continuous clinical management. Different viruses affect various organ systems, including the respiratory tract, liver, nervous system, and bloodstream. The immune system plays a crucial role in fighting viral infections, while vaccination remains one of the most effective strategies for preventing disease transmission and reducing global health burdens. The global virology market continues to expand due to increasing viral outbreaks, advancements in antiviral drug development, and growing vaccination programs. In 2025, the global viral infection treatment market is estimated to be valued at approximately USD 80 billion and is projected to reach nearly USD 135 billion by 2032, growing at a CAGR of around 6.7% during the forecast period.

The global viral infection therapeutics market is witnessing steady growth, driven by the rising prevalence of chronic and emerging viral diseases, increasing investments in vaccine research, and continuous advancements in antiviral therapies. HIV therapeutics continue to account for the largest market share, followed by treatments for influenza, hepatitis, respiratory viruses, and other emerging infections. North America remains the leading regional market due to its advanced healthcare infrastructure, strong pharmaceutical industry, and high treatment accessibility, while the Asia-Pacific region is expected to register the fastest growth owing to expanding healthcare investments, increasing awareness, and improved access to antiviral medications and vaccines. Furthermore, innovations in molecular diagnostics, combination therapies, personalized medicine, and AI-driven drug discovery are expected to accelerate the development of next-generation antiviral treatments and strengthen global preparedness against future viral outbreaks.

Bacterial Infections

Unlike viral infections, most bacterial infections can be effectively treated with antibiotics, although the growing emergence of antimicrobial resistance has become a major global health concern. While many bacterial species are beneficial and essential for maintaining human health, certain pathogenic bacteria cause infections affecting the respiratory, urinary, gastrointestinal, and bloodstream systems. Immunization plays a vital role in preventing several serious bacterial diseases, and ongoing research continues to focus on developing novel antibiotics, rapid diagnostic methods, antimicrobial stewardship strategies, and alternative therapies to combat drug-resistant bacteria. The global antibacterial drugs market continues to grow with the increasing burden of bacterial infections and antimicrobial resistance. In 2025, the global antibacterial drugs market is estimated to be valued at approximately USD 55.6 billion and is projected to reach nearly USD 67.9 billion by 2030, growing at a CAGR of around 4.1% during the forecast period.

The global bacterial infection treatment market is witnessing steady expansion, driven by the increasing incidence of hospital-acquired and community-acquired bacterial infections, rising awareness of antimicrobial resistance, and continuous investments in antibiotic research and development. Broad-spectrum antibiotics continue to account for a significant share of the market, while cephalosporins remain among the leading therapeutic classes. North America continues to dominate the global market due to its advanced healthcare infrastructure and strong pharmaceutical presence, whereas the Asia-Pacific region is expected to experience the fastest growth owing to increasing healthcare expenditure, expanding access to medical care, and government initiatives to address antibiotic resistance. Advances in precision diagnostics, next-generation antimicrobial agents, and innovative treatment approaches are expected to further drive market growth and improve the management of bacterial infections worldwide.

Fungal Infections

Fungal infections can range from superficial skin and nail infections to severe invasive diseases affecting internal organs, particularly in immunocompromised individuals. These infections trigger immune responses that may lead to inflammation and tissue damage if left untreated. Both topical and systemic antifungal therapies are widely used depending on the severity and type of infection. This session focuses on the latest advances in fungal disease diagnosis, antifungal drug development, resistance mechanisms, emerging fungal pathogens, and innovative treatment strategies. The global antifungal drugs market is estimated to be valued at approximately USD 17 billion in 2026 and is expected to witness steady growth, driven by the increasing prevalence of invasive fungal infections, rising incidence of immunocompromised patients, and growing awareness of fungal disease management.

The global fungal infection treatment market continues to evolve with increasing investments in novel antifungal therapies, rapid diagnostic technologies, and research targeting antifungal resistance. The emergence of drug-resistant fungal pathogens has accelerated the development of new drug classes with improved efficacy and safety profiles. Additionally, advancements in molecular diagnostics and precision medicine are enabling earlier detection and more targeted treatment approaches. North America remains a leading market due to its advanced healthcare infrastructure and strong research activities, while the Asia-Pacific region is expected to experience significant growth owing to expanding healthcare access, increasing disease awareness, and rising investments in infectious disease management

Virology and HIVAIDS

With the continuous emergence of new and evolving viral strains, virologists continue to play a critical role in advancing our understanding of viral diseases and developing innovative strategies for their prevention, diagnosis, and treatment. Among the most significant viral pathogens is Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV-1), a retrovirus responsible for Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome (AIDS), which primarily attacks the body's immune system. Despite remarkable progress in antiretroviral therapy (ART), HIV remains a major global public health challenge. While ART has dramatically reduced HIV-related mortality and improved the quality of life for millions, ongoing research is essential to achieve a functional cure, develop effective vaccines, and eliminate new infections.

We invite researchers, clinicians, healthcare professionals, and industry experts to join us in sharing their latest findings, innovations, and perspectives on this important and evolving field. Topics including HIV pathogenesis, antiviral drug development, vaccine research, diagnostic technologies, prevention strategies, and global public health initiatives will be central to the discussion.

Market Analysis:
The global HIV diagnostics market continues to experience strong growth, driven by increasing awareness, expanded screening programs, and technological advancements in diagnostic platforms. As of 2026, the market is estimated to be valued at approximately USD 3.6 billion and is projected to surpass USD 5.8 billion by 2031, growing at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of approximately 10% during the forecast period. This growth is largely attributed to the widespread adoption of point-of-care (POC) testing, rapid self-testing kits, molecular diagnostic technologies, and multiplex testing platforms capable of detecting multiple infectious diseases simultaneously.

In addition, global initiatives led by organizations such as UNAIDS, the World Health Organization (WHO), and national public health agencies continue to expand HIV testing and treatment programs, supporting the global goal of ending the HIV/AIDS epidemic as a public health threat. Increasing investments in research and development, digital health integration, and improved access to diagnostic services in low- and middle-income countries are further accelerating market growth and driving innovation across the HIV diagnostics landscape.

Antiviral Drug Resistance

Antiviral drug resistance occurs when viruses undergo genetic mutations that reduce or eliminate the effectiveness of antiviral medications. High mutation rates in viruses such as HIV, influenza, and hepatitis viruses, along with inappropriate or incomplete treatment, contribute to the emergence of resistant strains. Understanding the mechanisms of resistance is essential for developing next-generation antivirals, combination therapies, and effective treatment strategies to combat evolving viral infections.

Market Analysis:
The global antiviral drugs market continues to grow, driven by the increasing burden of viral diseases, rising investment in antiviral research, and the demand for innovative therapies. As of 2026, the market is estimated at approximately USD 69 billion and is projected to reach over USD 98 billion by 2032, growing at a CAGR of around 6%. Growth is supported by advancements in antiviral drug development, combination therapies, and expanding healthcare access, with North America leading the market while the Asia-Pacific region is expected to witness the fastest growth.

Catheter-Associated Urinary Tract Infection

Catheter-Associated Urinary Tract Infection (CAUTI) is one of the most common healthcare-associated infections (HAIs), occurring when microorganisms enter the urinary tract through an indwelling urinary catheter. The risk of infection increases with prolonged catheter use, making timely catheter removal, aseptic insertion techniques, proper hand hygiene, and closed drainage systems essential for prevention. Ongoing research focuses on improving infection control practices, antimicrobial-coated catheters, rapid diagnostics, and strategies to reduce antimicrobial resistance associated with CAUTIs.

Market Analysis:
The global CAUTI prevention and urinary catheter infection control market continues to expand due to increasing emphasis on reducing healthcare-associated infections and improving patient safety. As of 2026, the market is estimated at approximately USD 2.9 billion and is projected to exceed USD 4.5 billion by 2031, growing at a CAGR of around 9%. Market growth is driven by rising hospitalization rates, increasing adoption of antimicrobial-coated catheters, advancements in infection prevention technologies, and stronger healthcare policies worldwide, with North America leading the market and Asia-Pacific emerging as the fastest-growing region.

Hospital-Acquired Infections HAIs

Hospital-acquired infections (HAIs), also known as nosocomial infections, are infections that develop during a hospital stay, typically after 48 hours of admission. Common HAIs include bloodstream infections, urinary tract infections, surgical site infections, and pneumonia, often caused by multidrug-resistant organisms. This session will focus on infection prevention, antimicrobial stewardship, rapid diagnostics, surveillance, and innovative strategies to reduce the burden of HAIs and improve patient safety.

Market Analysis:
The global HAI prevention and control market continues to grow due to increasing awareness of infection prevention, rising antimicrobial resistance, and advancements in sterilization and diagnostic technologies. As of 2026, the market is estimated at approximately USD 34 billion and is projected to exceed USD 53 billion by 2031, growing at a CAGR of around 9%. Growth is driven by stronger infection control policies, expanding healthcare infrastructure, and increasing adoption of advanced monitoring and sterilization technologies, with North America leading the market and Asia-Pacific emerging as the fastest-growing region.

Maternal-Fetal Infections

Maternal-fetal infections, also known as congenital infections, occur when pathogens such as cytomegalovirus (CMV), toxoplasmosis, syphilis, HIV, or Zika virus are transmitted from mother to fetus during pregnancy or childbirth. These infections can lead to miscarriage, stillbirth, congenital abnormalities, and long-term health complications. This session will focus on prenatal screening, molecular diagnostics, maternal vaccination, prevention strategies, and advances in the diagnosis and management of maternal-fetal infections.

Market Analysis:
The global maternal-fetal infection diagnostics and therapeutics market continues to grow, driven by increasing awareness of prenatal care, advancements in molecular diagnostics, and expanding maternal screening programs. As of 2026, the market is estimated at approximately USD 4.3 billion and is projected to surpass USD 7 billion by 2031, growing at a CAGR of around 10%. Rising healthcare investments, improved access to prenatal care, and government initiatives supporting maternal and neonatal health are expected to fuel continued market growth, with North America leading the market and Asia-Pacific witnessing the fastest expansion.

Next-Generation Antibiotics

Next-generation antibiotics are innovative antimicrobial agents designed to combat multidrug-resistant (MDR) bacteria that no longer respond to conventional therapies. These advanced treatments employ novel mechanisms of action, including targeting bacterial virulence factors, microbiome modulation, and precision antimicrobial approaches, to improve treatment outcomes while addressing the growing threat of antimicrobial resistance (AMR). This session will explore recent advances in antibiotic discovery, novel therapeutics, resistance mechanisms, and strategies to combat drug-resistant infections.

Market Analysis:
The global next-generation antibiotics market is experiencing strong growth, driven by the rising burden of antimicrobial resistance, increasing investment in antibiotic research, and supportive regulatory initiatives. As of 2026, the market is estimated at approximately USD 6 billion and is projected to exceed USD 10.5 billion by 2031, growing at a CAGR of around 12%. Neurological and CNS Infections

Bacteria, viruses, fungi, and other infectious agents can affect the Central Nervous System (CNS), including the brain, spinal cord, and peripheral nerves, leading to severe neurological complications. While challenges such as over-the-counter antibiotic availability, uneven healthcare access, and rising antimicrobial consumption persist in several countries, the region's large patient population, expanding diagnostic capacity, and continuous healthcare reforms are expected to sustain long-term growth in infectious disease management and AMR innovation.

About City

“Once you experience the beauty and spirit of Paris, its memories remain with you forever, no matter where life takes you.”

Paris is one of the world’s most iconic and captivating cities, renowned for its rich history, cultural heritage, elegant architecture, and vibrant lifestyle. Located in the heart of the Île-de-France region in northern France, the city is beautifully crossed by the Seine River and surrounded by picturesque boulevards, gardens, and historic landmarks. Often referred to as the “City of Light” and the “City of Love,” Paris continues to attract millions of visitors each year with its timeless charm and global influence.

As one of the most visited destinations in the world, Paris welcomes travelers with world-famous attractions such as the Eiffel Tower, Louvre Museum, Notre-Dame Cathedral, Champs-Élysées, and Montmartre. The city’s cafés, art galleries, fashion houses, and lively cultural atmosphere make it a center for creativity, innovation, and inspiration.

Paris is also recognized for its multicultural environment, bringing together people from diverse backgrounds and making it one of Europe’s leading international cities. The city offers exceptional cuisine, ranging from traditional French delicacies to modern fine dining experiences, reflecting France’s worldwide reputation for culinary excellence.

In addition to its cultural significance, Paris is an important global hub for business, education, science, and technology. The city plays a major role in international commerce and sustainable innovation while maintaining its commitment to environmental preservation through its parks, green spaces, and urban sustainability initiatives.

With its unique blend of history, elegance, modernity, and global connectivity, Paris remains an inspiring destination for academic conferences, professional networking, tourism, and cultural exchange.
